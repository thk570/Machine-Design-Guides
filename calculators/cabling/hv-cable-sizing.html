Rating (service) = base rating × temperature factor × frequency factor × installation factor. Safety factor = rating (service) ÷ desired current — below 1.0 means the cable is undersized for the stated conditions.

Follows a 10 °C-halving (Arrhenius/Montsinger) ageing rule, anchored at 3000 h for a conductor held continuously at 150 °C: life = 3000 × 0.5(θc − 150)/10 hours. It estimates insulation thermal ageing only — it is not a fatigue, mechanical or partial-discharge life estimate.

Transient overload check

The rating above is for continuous current; a brief overload doesn't last long enough to reach that steady state. This checks whether the conductor's thermal mass can absorb it without exceeding the insulation's short-time temperature limit5.

θ0 is the conductor temperature just before the event — θc at full continuous load is the worst case. θmax defaults to 250 °C (XLPE/EPR); PVC is typically 160 °C5 — check the cable datasheet's short-circuit rating. "Occurs" is 0 for a one-off event.

Peak transient temperature, θt

θt = (θ0 + 234.5)·exp[(Ieff√t / (226·S))²] − 234.5 — the adiabatic copper-conductor equation, which accounts for resistance rising with temperature5. S is the nominal cross-section; Ieff = It ÷ frequency factor, so AC skin effect is carried over from the rating above. It ignores heat lost to the insulation, so it is conservative for longer pulses, and it models one isolated pulse.

Source & method

This page reproduces the calculation logic of an internal HV cable sizing spreadsheet (a traction/pump HV cable sizing tool), not a single published standard. The pieces of that method:

  • Base current ratings (std. conditions) and cable dimensions — manufacturer datasheet figures carried over from the source workbook (Coroflex 9-2611 series, Radox and Radox FLEX screened single-core HV cable).
  • Ambient-temperature correction factor — the classic form used in IEC 60287 current-rating calculations, factor = √((θc − θa) ⁄ (θc,ref − θa,ref)), evaluated here with θc,ref = 150 °C and θa,ref = 30 °C fixed by the source workbook.
  • Grouping/installation derating by number of cables and installation method — the same style of factor as IEC 60364-5-52 grouping tables.
  • Conductor thermal life vs. temperature — a 10 °C-halving Arrhenius/Montsinger approximation, a common rule of thumb for polymer insulation ageing, not itself a standard.
  • Transient overload — the adiabatic short-circuit heating equation for copper conductors (the basis of the IEC 60949 / IEC 60364-5-54 k-factor method), not taken from the source workbook.

This page does not replace the source spreadsheet or the cable manufacturers' datasheets — for a specific programme, check current figures against the datasheet revision actually procured. Figures throughout are for indication only.

Applicable design standards

  • IEC 60287 — Electric cables — Calculation of the current rating.
  • IEC 60364-5-52 — Low-voltage electrical installations — Part 5-52: Selection and erection of electrical equipment — Wiring systems.
  • IEC 60949 — Calculation of thermally permissible short-circuit currents, taking into account non-adiabatic heating effects.

This page does not reproduce these standards' own tables or calculation procedure in full; conformance-critical cable sizing should refer to the standards themselves and to the cable manufacturer's datasheet.
