Busbar SizingElectrical
Current-density method ·

Bare rectangular busbar cross-section from a target current density, plus a suggested width/thickness (X/Y) split with a skin-effect check for AC, and a transient-overload check for a brief current above the continuous rating. Materials come from the shared library6; the current-density defaults and aspect-ratio presets are this page's own rules of thumb, not a reproduction of a standard's sizing tables.

1. Inputs

Materials from the shared library (data/materials.js) — filtered to conductor grades (those with a documented %IACS conductivity). Default is C101 electrolytic tough pitch copper6.

For AC, I is RMS — heating follows I²R. Peak current isn't used here; it only matters for a separate short-circuit-force check.

A = I ÷ J. Frequency doesn't change A — skin effect instead affects how it should be shaped, checked in section 3.

3. Aspect Ratio & Dimensions

X ≥ Y by convention — wider and thinner sheds heat better and, for AC, stays closer to a skin depth4. Above ~20–25 mm thickness, prefer laminated bars.

4. Transient Overload Check

Section 2 sizes for continuous current; a brief overload doesn't last long enough to reach that steady state. This instead checks whether the bar's thermal mass can absorb it, against a bare-copper short-time limit7.

Peak transient temperature, θt

ΔT = It² ρ t / (c × density × A²) — the adiabatic short-circuit thermal-withstand method7,8, conservative for longer pulses. It models one isolated pulse; a duty cycle repeating faster than the bar can cool needs an RMS-equivalent current instead.

Applicable design standards

  • IEC 60943 — Guidance concerning the permissible temperature rise for parts of electrical equipment, in particular for terminals.
  • IEC 61439-1 — Low-voltage switchgear and controlgear assemblies — Part 1: General rules (temperature-rise limits for bare conductors in an assembly).
  • IEC 60287-1-1 — Electric cables — Calculation of the current rating — Part 1-1: AC resistance / skin and proximity effect provisions, the basis for the skin-depth check in section 3.
  • IEC 60865-1 — Short-circuit currents — Calculation of effects — Part 1: Definitions and calculation methods (the adiabatic thermal-withstand method behind section 4).

This page uses simplified rules of thumb, not these standards' own tables. Conformance- or safety-critical design should go to the full standards directly.
