Screw TorquesFasteners & Torque
Metric · coarse thread only ·

Recommended tightening torque for a metric coarse-thread bolt or screw. Two very different joints share this page: a bolt running into a properly rated nut or insert (governed by the bolt's proof/yield strength), and a screw threaded directly into a tapped hole in the parent material (normally governed by thread stripping in that material, with a check that the screw itself isn't the weaker element). Both use the same underlying nut-factor torque relationship T = K·F·d1,2, but the axial load F behind it comes from a different place in each case.

1. Joint Type

2. Bolt & Retention

%

The property class selected is the bolt / screw; preload and torque are based on its proof stress, per ISO 898-1 for steel3 (8.8 splits at M16: 580 MPa ≤M16, 600 MPa >M16; 9.8 is only standardised up to M16) and ISO 3506-1 Rp0.2 for stainless4,5. The mating nut is specified one step down in designation — the nut class equal to the bolt's first number (e.g. class 8 nut for an 8.8 bolt)10 — so the bolt, not the nut thread, is the weaker element. 75% of proof load is a common conservative starting point; up to ~90% is used for well-controlled tightening methods6.

2. Screw & Parent Material

Materials pulled from the shared library (data/materials.js) — filtered to steel, stainless and aluminum, the families this method's shear-strength assumption is validated for.

mm

Based on NASA/TM—2017-219475's noncritical-joint method9: pullout load from the parent material's thread-shear area, assembly torque taken as 65% of the torque implied by that pullout load (a built-in safety factor of ~1.54, on top of a factor of 3 already in the pullout formula). The resulting assembly preload is then checked against the screw: if it exceeds 90% of the screw's proof load, the screw is the limiting element and the torque is capped to that limit. Thread stripping and screw tension only — no structural or vibrational load, and not for flight or safety-critical hardware.

Stress area uses the standard simplified formula As = (π/4)·(d − 0.9382·P)²7. Torque wrenches are typically accurate to only about ±10%, so treat the result as the centre of a band rather than an exact value6.

Pitch diameter dm = d − 0.6495·P. Pullout load P = π·dm·Fsu·L / 3, where Fsu is the parent material's assumed ultimate shear strength (0.6× ultimate tensile for steel, 0.55× for stainless/aluminum) and the /3 is a built-in safety factor9. Assembly preload = 0.65·P, capped at 0.9·Fp where Fp = proof stress × As for the selected screw class.

Nut factor K lumps thread and under-head friction into one coefficient: steel dry K = 0.20, steel lubricated K = 0.15, stainless dry K = 0.30 (galling risk — see warning above), stainless lubricated (anti-seize) K = 0.208. Real friction varies with plating, surface finish and reuse, so K can easily be ±30% off a generic table for a specific batch of fasteners2. Applicable design standards

  • ISO 898-1:2013 — Mechanical properties of fasteners made of carbon steel and alloy steel — Part 1: Bolts, screws and studs with specified property classes.
  • ISO 898-2:2022 — Mechanical properties of fasteners made of carbon steel and alloy steel — Part 2: Nuts with specified property classes (bolt/nut class pairing).
  • ISO 3506-1:2020 — Fasteners — Mechanical properties of corrosion-resistant stainless steel fasteners — Part 1: Bolts, screws and studs.
  • ISO 3506-2:2020 — Fasteners — Mechanical properties of corrosion-resistant stainless steel fasteners — Part 2: Nuts.
  • VDI 2230 Blatt 1 — Systematic calculation of highly stressed bolted joints — Joints with one cylindrical bolt (the rigorous basis for preload/embedding/torque scatter that this page simplifies).
  • ISO 10511:2012 — Prevailing torque type hexagon thin nuts (with non-metallic insert) — governs nyloc-type lock nuts; rivnuts/threaded inserts and self-clinching nuts are generally manufacturer-specific parts without a single governing ISO standard.

This page does not reproduce the full ISO 898-1/-2 / ISO 3506-1/-2 property-class tables, VDI 2230's joint-diagram method (elastic interaction, embedding loss, torque scatter), or the NASA report's complete table set. A joint with fatigue loading, a soft-gasketed or non-metallic clamped stack, an insert/clinch-nut whose pull-out rating the manufacturer publishes separately, or any safety-critical or flight duty should be verified against the actual standard/source or a full VDI 2230 calculation, not this simplified estimate. The edge-proximity knockdown in section 3 is a general approximation, not a cited standard method.
