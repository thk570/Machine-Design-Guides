Metric · coarse & fine threads ·

Recommended tightening torque for a metric bolt or screw, coarse or fine pitch1. Two very different joints share this page: a bolt running into a properly rated nut or insert (governed by the bolt's proof/yield strength, or by PEM's published table for self-clinching nuts), and a screw threaded directly into a tapped hole in the parent material (normally governed by thread stripping in that material, with a check that the screw itself isn't the weaker element). Both use the same underlying nut-factor torque relationship T = K·F·d2,3, but the axial load F behind it comes from a different place in each case.

1. Joint Type

2. Bolt & Retention

%

The property class selected is the bolt / screw; preload and torque are based on its proof stress, per ISO 898-1 for steel4 (8.8 splits at M16: 580 MPa ≤M16, 600 MPa >M16; 9.8 is only standardised up to M16) and ISO 3506-1 Rp0.2 for stainless5,6. The mating nut is specified one step down in designation — the nut class equal to the bolt's first number (e.g. class 8 nut for an 8.8 bolt)7 — so the bolt, not the nut thread, is the weaker element. 75% of proof load is a common conservative starting point; up to ~90% is used for well-controlled tightening methods8.

Based on NASA/TM—2017-219475's noncritical-joint method9: pullout load from the parent material's thread-shear area, with assembly preload set as a percentage of it (default 65%, on top of a factor of 3 already in the pullout formula). If that exceeds 90% of the screw's proof load, the screw is the limiting element and the torque is capped. Thread stripping and screw tension only, and not for flight or safety-critical hardware.

Stress area uses the standard simplified formula As = (π/4)·(d − 0.9382·P)²10. Torque wrenches are typically accurate to only about ±10%, so treat the result as the centre of a band rather than an exact value8.

Pullout load P = π·dm·Fsu·L / 3, with dm = d − 0.6495·P and Fsu the parent material's assumed ultimate shear strength (0.6× ultimate tensile for steel, 0.55× for stainless/aluminum); the /3 is a built-in safety factor9. Assembly preload = pullout %·P (default 65%), capped at 0.9·Fp where Fp = proof stress × As for the selected screw class. L is capped at 2×d.

Nut factor K lumps thread and under-head friction into one coefficient: steel dry K = 0.20, lubricated 0.15; stainless dry 0.30 (galling risk — see warning above), lubricated (anti-seize) 0.2011. Applicable design standards

  • ISO 261:1998 — ISO general purpose metric screw threads — General plan (coarse and fine pitch series).
  • ISO 262:2023 — ISO general purpose metric screw threads — Selected sizes for bolts, screws, studs and nuts.
  • ISO 898-1:2013 — Mechanical properties of fasteners made of carbon steel and alloy steel — Part 1: Bolts, screws and studs with specified property classes.
  • ISO 898-2:2022 — Mechanical properties of fasteners made of carbon steel and alloy steel — Part 2: Nuts with specified property classes (bolt/nut class pairing).
  • ISO 3506-1:2020 — Fasteners — Mechanical properties of corrosion-resistant stainless steel fasteners — Part 1: Bolts, screws and studs.
  • ISO 3506-2:2020 — Fasteners — Mechanical properties of corrosion-resistant stainless steel fasteners — Part 2: Nuts.
  • ISO 7719 — Prevailing torque type all-metal hexagon regular nuts — Property classes 5, 8 and 10 (Stover / DIN 980-type nuts; 2012 edition superseded by ISO 7719:2025).
  • ISO 10511:2012 — Prevailing torque type hexagon thin nuts (with non-metallic insert) — governs nyloc-type lock nuts; rivnuts/threaded inserts and self-clinching nuts are manufacturer-specific parts without a single governing ISO standard (PEM values here come from PEM's own data).
  • ISO 2320:2015 — Fasteners — Prevailing torque steel nuts — Functional properties (first-installation maximum prevailing torque; non-metallic insert nuts limited to 50% of the all-metal value).
  • VDI 2230 Blatt 1 — Systematic calculation of highly stressed bolted joints — Joints with one cylindrical bolt (the rigorous basis for preload/embedding/torque scatter that this page simplifies).

This page does not reproduce the full ISO 898-1/-2 / ISO 3506-1/-2 property-class tables, VDI 2230's joint-diagram method (elastic interaction, embedding loss, torque scatter), or the NASA report's complete table set. A joint with fatigue loading, a soft-gasketed or non-metallic clamped stack, an insert/clinch-nut whose pull-out rating the manufacturer publishes separately, or any safety-critical or flight duty should be verified against the actual standard/source or a full VDI 2230 calculation, not this simplified estimate. The edge-proximity knockdown in section 3 is a general approximation, not a cited standard method.
