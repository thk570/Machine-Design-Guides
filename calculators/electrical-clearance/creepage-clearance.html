1. System & Insulation Conditions

Rated impulse withstand voltage

V

Sets the rated impulse withstand voltage (Uimp) from the supply's nominal voltage and how far downstream the insulation sits1. For a DC bus (e.g. a battery pack) enter the DC voltage directly against the same table — the standard applies the bands identically to a.c. and d.c. supplies1.

Altitude

m

Clearances are defined for altitudes up to 2000 m; above that, lower air density reduces the breakdown voltage of a given gap, so the required clearance is scaled up by an altitude correction factor2. Creepage distances are not altitude-corrected.

Working voltage, pollution degree & material group

V

The working voltage across a specific insulation gap can differ from the mains nominal voltage above (e.g. an isolated HV DC bus fed from a lower-voltage a.c. supply) — enter the actual rms or DC voltage across the gap being sized. It sets the creepage distance directly, and — via pollution degree — the minimum clearance floor alongside the impulse-voltage clearance above.

Substrate materials and their typical CTI are pulled from the shared Material Library3 (data/materials.js) — fillers, glass reinforcement and flame retardants can pull a specific grade's CTI well below the typical figure shown, so confirm against that grade's own datasheet before relying on it.

Insulation level

Functional and basic/supplementary insulation use the table values as looked up. Reinforced insulation (a single insulation system giving the same protection as basic + supplementary) steps the clearance up to the next value in the standard's impulse-voltage series, and doubles the creepage distance4.

Applicable design standards

  • IEC 60664-1 — Insulation coordination for equipment within low-voltage systems — Part 1: Principles, requirements and tests
  • IEC 60112 — Method for the determination of the proof and the comparative tracking indices of solid insulating materials (defines the CTI values behind the material groups above)

This page reproduces a subset of Annex F's tables (impulse voltage, clearance, altitude correction, creepage) for the single most common design path — it does not cover the standard's full scope (uncoated/coated PCBs, functional insulation exceptions, partial-discharge testing, or voltages above 1500 V/1000 V rms). Conformance-critical work should check the actual standard.
